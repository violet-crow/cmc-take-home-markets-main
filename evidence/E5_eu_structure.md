# E5: Europe: procedures and scope

Facts only. The consequences for the model are yours to draw.

- **One variations framework.** Commission Regulation (EC) No 1234/2008, as amended by Delegated Regulation (EU) 2024/1701 (applicable 1 January 2025), defines the variation types IA, IA_IN, IB and II and the extension. Commission Guideline C/2025/5045 (applicable 15 January 2026) details the classification. The European Commission adopts these acts; the European Medicines Agency (EMA) publishes the procedural guidance for centrally authorised products.
- **Four routes to a licence.** The centralised procedure (one marketing authorisation, granted by the European Commission on EMA's opinion, valid in every member state); the mutual-recognition and decentralised procedures (national licences in several member states, coordinated through CMDh); and purely national procedures. The same variation types apply on every route.
- **Member states.** 27 EU member states, each with a national competent authority: for example BfArM in Germany and ANSM in France.
- **EEA.** Norway, Iceland and Liechtenstein apply the EU pharmaceutical rules through the EEA Agreement. A centralised authorisation covers them.
- **United Kingdom.** Since 1 January 2021 the MHRA grants UK licences separately; since 1 January 2025 that includes Northern Ireland. The MHRA's variation categories reuse the EU numbering (IA, IA_IN, IB, II).
- **Switzerland.** Swissmedic grants Swiss licences separately. Its categories also reuse the EU numbering.
- **Beyond Europe.** Saudi Arabia's SFDA and South Africa's SAHPRA also publish categories numbered IA / IB / II.
